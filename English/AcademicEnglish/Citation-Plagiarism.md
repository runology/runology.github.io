<aside>
💡 Plagiarism: Using words or ideas of other writers without giving proper acknowledgement.
**Plagiarism refers to the unauthorized use of articles or viewpoints from other authors, which can be avoided by improving time management, understanding the consequences of plagiarism, studying curriculum regulations, using citation formats, learning how to paraphrase, and strengthening academic integrity.**
</aside>

- widespread problem in higher education
- historically viewed as an academic crime
- the term textural borrowing sometimes used to remove negative connotations.
- most universities use plagiarism detection tools, such as Turnitin

Intent:

- intentional plagiarism:

    also called, academic or textual plagiarism

### Why do students commit plagiarism? How can students avoid it?

1. time problems
    
    Reason:
    
    - starting work assignment too late
    - too many deadlines
    
    Solutions:
    
    - improve time management
    - ask tutor for extension

2. Lack of Understanding
    
    Reasons:
    
    - may not know copying is wrong
    - Rules may be different in other countries
    
    Solutions:
    
    - understand plagiarism may lead to serious consequences. e.g. 0 score
    - Learn the regulations for your course

3. Lack of citation skills
    
    Reasons:
    
    - need to acknowledge writer with in-text citation
    
    Solutions:
    
    - familiarize yourself with requirements, like, MLA, APA, Harvard?
    - Keep track of sources （which info come from where）

4. Lack of paraphrasing
    
    Reasons:
    
    - may only change a few words
    - notes are copied, note paraphrased
    
    Solutions:
    
    - learn how to paraphrase, changing words, word form and word order
    - Take notes using your own words
    - use quotation marks for all quotes

5. Lack of academic honesty
    
    Reason:
    
    - knowing it is wrong, but do it anyway
    
    Solutions:
    
    - understand that academic integrity is important in the academic community.
    - realize that plagiarism will be detected by tools

# How to paraphrase effectively

## What is paraphrasing?

- Representing ideas and information **in your own words** and acknowledging where they come from.
    
    By using your own words, you demonstrate your understanding and your ability to convey your information.
    
- Paraphrasing shows you have a clear **understanding** of the topic
- Paraphrasing ensures that the text used is relevant to your specific **assignment**.
- Direct **copying** is plagiarism. Quotation is acceptable but only when defining terms or a very central statement.
- 10% quotation rule.
    
    no more than 10% of the total word count of the paper or essay.
    
    Examples:
    
![Untitled](../_images/Citation%20&%20Plagiarism%2069904b0f445b43ec872f6c9f68a41fbd/Untitled%2028.png)

![Untitled](../_images/Citation%20&%20Plagiarism%2069904b0f445b43ec872f6c9f68a41fbd/Untitled%2029.png)

Poor Reasons:

- Not in your own words
- Plagiarism scan shows very similar to the original text.

### 4 Key Stages of Paraphrasing

- Highlight important words/items （that cannot be changed）
- Find synonyms/alternative words for high frequency words
- Change grammar:  Structure, active to passive, nouns to verbs, adj to adv, word order
- Check meaning.

### 2. Reference Format

**In text referencing - quotations** include the author's surname, year of publication and page number or page range or paragraph number.

**In text referencing- paraphrasing:** when paraphrasing or summarizing ideas or opinions of others, include the author's surname and year of publication at the end of your writing.

If you use the author's name in the sentence you write the year of publication in round brackets immediately after the author's surname. Example: Johnson (2014).

When you have more than one author you cite both authors every time the reference occurs. Example: (Levasseur & Carrier, 2013).

If the text referencing has three or more authors include only the surname of the first author followed by et al. Example: (McCune et al., 2016)

Reference list includes only the sources used within the body of your writing.

References are arranged in alphabetical order, and must be indent 5 – 7 spaces.
