# Clauses

clause is sentence.

A clause is a group of words that functions as one part of speech,

and that includes a **Subject** and a **Verb**.

### Independent Clauses （complete sentence）

- Complete thought  (单独拆出来，有明确的含义)

e.g. *Sally sings*

### Dependent Clauses (Incomplete sentence)

- can’t **Stand Alone** , doesn’t make any sense.

e.g. :

*because he wanted to find out the answer.*

*If you work hard,* 

Here, because is a subordinator.

---

### Noun clauses

It expresses sth that cannot be expressed by a single noun or a noun phase.

e.g.: *We can eat whatever you want.*

 *I know **why** you were angry.*  

 *I know **how** you did it.* 

 *I know **where** you were yesterday.*  

 *I know **what** they will do.* 

 *I know **who** will win the race.*  

 *I know **when** she will arrive.*

### Adjective Clauses

Boost your IELTS score, improve your speaking **accuracy**.

e.g.: 

*The boy who sits there is my son.*

*The boy of whom we’re discussing is my son.*

**Where** - mark place (can be omitted, but must be replaced by a preposition)

e.g.:

*The school **where** he teaches is very old.*

*The school he teaches **in** is very old.*

### Essential Clause & Non-essential Clause

Essential Clause(定语从句)：The clause is **essential**. Without it, the sentence meaning is **vague**.

Non-essential Clause (非定语从句): Purely extra info to add more interest or flavor to the sentence but **not necessary** to understand the sentence.

e.g.: 

*The boy **who sits there** is my son. (remove clause, the sentence is vague, we don’t know which is son)*

*Tom**, who sits there,** is my son.* (Tom is a specific person, without clause, the sentence make clear sense.)

# Cohesive Device

<aside>
💡 conjunctions = cohesive device = transition

- **Conjunctions**: Words that connect clauses or sentences, such as "and," "but," "or," "because," and "although."
- **Cohesive Devices**: A broader category that includes conjunctions, as well as other words and phrases that help to create cohesion in a text. This includes pronouns, determiners, conjunctions, and lexical ties (e.g., repetition of key terms).
- **Transition Words**: These are specific types of cohesive devices used to link sentences and paragraphs smoothly. Examples include "however," "therefore," "moreover," and "consequently."
- **Linking Words**: Another term for words that connect ideas, often used interchangeably with "transition words." Examples include "also," "furthermore," "on the other hand," and "in addition."
- **Cohesion**: logic among sentences

## Simple Sentences

Just **one independent clause** by itself.

The verb must agree with the subject and show the tense of the sentence.

e.g.: *Sally kicked the ball.*

## Compound Sentences

- **independent clause +** , **+  coordinating conjunction independent clause**
- **independent clause + ; + independent clause**
- **Coordinating Conjunctions** : **, + FANBOYS**
    
     ***(**For + And + Nor + But + Or + Yet + So)*
    

e.g.:  *I like tea**, but** I prefer coffee.*

*I like tea; I prefer coffee. （Use ”;” to replace “, FANBOYS”; One sentence only allows one ;）*

## Complex Sentences

- **one independent clause** and **at least one dependent clause**
- joined by **Subordinating Conjunctions**
    - Subordinating Conjunction used to link **dependent** clauses to **independent** clause.
    - dependent clause must begin with subordinating conjunction

e.g. *Although it was raining, I walked to work.*

**2 Types** of complex sentences are possible

- Independent clause + dependent clause.

e.g.: *I walked to work **although** it was raining. （no comma needed）*

- dependent clause + comma + independent clause.

e.g.: *Although it was raining, I walked to work.*

## Complex and Compound Sentences

- **at least two independent** clauses and at least **one dependent clause**.
- a **mix** of complex and compound sentences

e.g.:

*The dog barked loudly, and she ran around the room as her owner tried to relax with his newspaper.*

### **run-on** **sentences**

连写句: independent clauses are joined incorrectly

e.g.: *I’m tired~~, therefore~~ I go to sleep.* 

- Use a period (full stop “.”):  *I’m tired**.** I go to sleep.*
- Use a semicolon: *I’m tired**;** I go to sleep.*
- Use , + coordinating conjunction:  *I’m tired**, so** I go to sleep.*
- Use a subordinating conjunction: ***Because** I’m tired, I go to sleep.*

# Parallelism

Parts of a sentence have the same features.

1. Keep nouns with nouns
    
    e.g.: Incorrect: *He enjoys football more than ~~playing~~ baseball.*
    
2. Keep verbs with verbs, and keep them in the same tense
    
    e.g., Incorrect: *Last weekend we ran, swam, and ~~went bowling.~~*
    
3. Keep adjectives with adjectives
    
    e.g., Incorrect: *The movie was fascinating and ~~informed me about a lot of things~~*. (*informative* is possible)
    
4. Articles(冠词) and prepositions(介词) must apply to the first item or all items. 
    
    e.g., Incorrect: *The Chinese, ~~the~~ Japanese, Koreans, and Mongolians all come from East Asia.*
    
    Incorrect: *He looked in the fridge, on the counter, and (**on the**) stovetop for his dinner.* (all the items need advice “on the”)
    

- Some other **Incorrect Examples:**

*He is good at studying English, ~~in ****~~history ~~class~~, and French.*

*Buying an iPhone is almost as expensive as (**buying)** a new laptop. (Original sentence lack buying)*

*She’d prefer to learn English than **failing** her exam. (fail)*

- Incorrect Long sentences for the sake of **different tense and grammar** in two clauses.

*As a general trend, the chart shows that **while** the unemployment rate  in the United States gradually decreased over the period shown, ~~in Japan there was a significant increase.~~→ it increased significantly in Japan.*

*According to the data from the graph, in March 1993, 7% of Americans were unemployed, whereas ~~the unemployment rate in Japan  was just 2.4%.~~ → whereas just 2.4% of Japan were unemployed.*

# Punctuation

### , Comma

1. to join two independent clauses **with FANBOYS**
    
    e.g., *I am tired, but I don’t wanna sleep.*
    
2. after an introductory phrase or when a complex sentence begins with the dependent clause
    
    e.g., *When the boys came home from school, they were alway hungry.*
    
    *Long age, there was a dog.*
    
3. Separate items: e.g., *I’ve been to Germany, France, Spain, and Italy.*
4. Separate non-essential information within a sentence
    
    e.g., *Paul, whose father is a government officer, deliberately hit the girl.*
    
5. after a conjunctive verb or transitional element
    
    e.g., ***Therefore,** he was allowed to play video game.*
    
6. use commas with quotes
    
    e.g., *“Get out,” she said.*
    
7. qualifications and positions when describing people
    
    e.g., *Judy, MD. （Doctor of Medicine 职业）*
    

**, split errors**

- two independent clauses are joined by a comma
    
    e.g.: *~~We failed, therefore, we lost.~~* (This is a run-on sentence, caz comma cannot join two independent sentences)
    
    e.g: *~~He is tired, he continues to run.~~*
    
    - end the first and start new one
    - use a semicolon
    - use a coordinating conjunction

### . Period (full stop)

at the end of a sentence.

A period should always be followed by a capital letter.

### ! Exclamation Mark

never use it in IELTS Test

### ; Semi-colons

two uses: 

- separating long items in a list
    
    e.g.：
    
    Incorrect: *There are many types: ~~verbs; nouns; adjectives.~~→ There are may types: verbs, nouns, and adjectives.*
    
    Correct*: There are many types: verbs, which indicate actions; nouns, which identify objects; adjectives, which describe qualities.*
    
- joining 2 closely-related independent clauses: (**in compound sentences**)
    
    *e.g.: We failed; therefore, we lost the prize. (2 independent clauses)*
    
    *There was a big traffic jam; cars were barely moving around town.*
    

### : Colons

one use:

- introducing a list or other additional explanation, definition, etc. comes after an independent clause
    
    *e.g.: There are 3 main types of errors: comma splice errors, run-on sentences and fragments. →（what are they?）*
    
    *e.g.: The reason is clear: illness. → (what is it?)*

### Incorrect Examples

Passive Voice is quite formal, and not all words can be used in this voice.

Can not use transitive verbs with the passive

e.g., *~~Paris was visited by me. The food was enjoyed by me, and the Effiel Tower was seen by me.~~*

### Participle Clause

- increase the degree of formality
- begin with a present or past participle
- Causes and Solutions

e.g., ***Started in 1979,** China’s One Child Policy was aimed at reducing the rate of population growth.*

*Unwilling to move to the cities, many old people are left in the countryside with little financial support.*

*Unable to find a job*, *the former prisoners return to a life of crime.*

Relationship between two parts must bee **clear**.

Make sure that the first word in the sentence refers  to the subject in the main clause or else you would end up with a dangling participle:

e.g.,

***~~Causing** a great deal of pollution, **people** often use cars to drive to work*.~~ (People不是造成pollution的原因，car才是)

*Causing a great deal of pollution, cars are still the main choice for people getting to work.*

尽管没有语义错误，但两个句子部分的关系不是很明确，句子不够清晰，应尽量避免这样用，最好加上Transition words

*Cars are still the main choice, despite causing a great deal of pollution.*
