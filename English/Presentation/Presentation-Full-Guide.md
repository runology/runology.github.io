### Body

- present **important data/material** to prove your point
- use **signpost phrases** to help you audience follw
- draw attentions to **important points**
    - ask a question “I want you to think about this for a moment.”
- plan **visual aids** to reinforce the verbal message

### Conclusion

- review your **purpose** of the presentation
- give a brief **summary**
- give a final **take-away message (离席后仍能记住这个观点)**

### Q&A

- invite questions
- think of questions
- research the topic widely before the presentation
- understand the question: repeat it
- don't be afraid to say you don't know, and tell them you will answer it later.

TIPS:

- minimum questions: 3-7
- Never ask loaded questions? (倾向性， easy)
- Never ask yes/no questions
- Always ask Wh-questions
- Never ask about sensitive issue? (Political, Sexual…)

# Delivery of a Presentation

### Practise:

THe more your practise, the more familar you will become with the content of your presentation, the more fluent and natural your delivery is likely to be.

### Pace:

- speak a little more slowly than you normally do, if you feel nervous or worried about your pronouciation.
- listening is a difficult skill, your audience need surfficient time to grasp your points.
- the larger you audience, the more slowly you should speak
- vary the speed, speak slowly when the content is important.
- avoid speaking quickly to rush through and finish your presentation.

### Volume

- speaking a little louder than you think is necessary, ensuring your voice is projected to the back of the room.
- avoid speaking too quietly.

### Emphasis

Add stress to the important words.

make it easier for the audience to follow 

- speak the word more slowly
- speak the word more loudly
- pause briefly before and after the word.
- use stress for important points and contrasts

# Body Language of Presentation

**Body Language = Nonverbal Communication**

The message you give with your body is as important as the one you give using words.

Body Language For Presentation, involves many diff elements. The most important ones are : posture, position, movement, eye contact. Each is going to be illustrated in more details below.

### Posture

Standing well allows you to control your breathing and voice production.

- stand up straight
- hold your hands in front of your body or behind your back
- avoid putting your hands in your pockets

### Position

- face the audience
- avoid talking if your back is turned
- avoid talking to the whiteboard, PowerPoint Screen, or your notes.

### Movement

- use gestures to stress important points
- use hands to demostrate or to count
- avoid moving too much , as this is distracting
- avoid standing completely still, as this is boring.
- avoid nervous gestures such as touchingn your hair or glasses.

### Eye contact

It helps the speak to relax, and encourages the audience to listen.

- look at the audience as much as you can
- include all members of the audience
- avoid focusing on just one side of the room
- avoid focusing on just one person
- avoid talking to your visual aids or notes

other social **etiquette**

### Space

keep a Social distance

### Touch

Never Touching anybody.

No hugs, no handshake.

It's useful to remember that looking confident and feeling confident are not the same. Almost everyone gets nervous when they have to speak in public ,But not everyone shows it. Use strong body language, including good posture, ,,,,,to help you appear confident.

# Design a PPT

## Improve PPT By Avoiding the Pitfalls of bad Slides

- Backgroud/Design
    - academic and professional
    - design reflects presentation
- Fonts / colors
    - title 38-44 , main body 28, references 18
    - avoid capitalizing，only recommend Times New Roman, never use other fonts
    - color only: black blue white red
- Structure
    - use 1-2 slides per minutes
    - write in point form, not complete sentences
    - 4-5 points per slide
    - avoid wordiness:
        - use key words and phrases only;
        - use nouns only if possible
        - take out accurate data
- Animation
    - use ‘appear’ only
- Pictures
    - need permission to use photos
    - use photo companies that allow non-business use/royalty free photos
    - no irrelevant pic
    - no baby / cartoons / jokes
- Graphs / Data/ Stats
    - not complicated
    - if points and graph are all required, put graph in next slide
- Referencing
    - APA
- spelling and grammar
    - proofread your slide for
        - spelling mistakes
        - the use of repeated words
        - grammatical errors
    - let someone check you presentation

### **PowerPoint Presentation Tips:**

1. **10 – 12** Slides (not including , references  and Q&A)

2. Well organized

3. Font size **(Title: 36 – 44 / Main Point: 28 / In-text citation & Reference: 18)**

4. High quality pictures

5. Add Slide number

6. **7 references** (Books / Article / Online Magazines) (No Wikipedia + No Blogs + No Social Media)

7. No cheesy pictures

8. No video

9. Do not overload data in one slide

10. **No paragraphs** (Only Key points)

11. No details

12. No more than 1 or 2 pics in one slide

13. **Use pie chart / table / bar chart**

14. Use animations (Not Too Much)

15. No sound

16. Font: Times New Roman & Arial

17. Never plagiarise (Copy & Paste the data) (Always paraphrase the ideas in your own words)

18. Never forget to include the in-text citation for the images & data in each slide

19. Slide 1 should include your topic + your full name + Teacher’s name + Course info

20. Include one slide for agenda / overview (Mostly the second slide is the outline)

21. Include one slide for Question & Answer (Wh-Questions are recommended)

22. The last slide must be the references (APA format)

23. Never include more than four bullet points in one slide

24. Manage your time (**1 minute** is recommended for each slide)

25. Practice Makes Perfect
